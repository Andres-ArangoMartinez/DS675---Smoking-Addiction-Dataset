# Related-work source collection

This folder collects sources for subsequent review and deeper development of our project's background and related work. It is an expandable reference collection, not a completed systematic review or an adopted experimental protocol. Follow the original links to check methods, measurements, access conditions, and results before citing or adapting a source.

**Research date:** November 10, 2026. **Collection prepared and selected citation metadata checked:** November 11, 2026.

**Project context:** [Project overview](../../../README.md) · [Current methodology and Kate's proposed extension](../../../docs/methodology.md) · [Dataset access and pending audit](../../../data/README.md).

The project's proposal describes synthetic longitudinal data with 1,000 participants, 30 days, 30,000 records, and 35 columns. Those characteristics, the original generation process, exact Kaggle version, license, score definitions, and file hashes still require verification against the selected download. The research search did not independently establish the Kaggle data's provenance. See the [Kaggle source](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/datascikhan/the-nicotine-craving-and-anxiety-dynamics-dataset/data).

**Reading guide:** Results below are reported by their sources, not reproduced by our team. “Requires verification” identifies missing evidence; “proposed” identifies plans rather than completed analyses. Momentary and daily ratings, cigarettes and nicotine pouches, and current-state classification and future prediction are not interchangeable. Metrics from different outcomes and evaluation designs should not be ranked against one another.

| Work / Dataset and Authors | Brief Description: Methods and Results | Research Objective |

| [EMASENS: EMA and wearable-sensor data](https://zenodo.org/records/12532026) — Olga Perski and collaborators; archived release v2.0.0, 2024. [Code/data repository](https://github.com/OlgaPerski/EMASENS_ML) · [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0000594). | **Real, completed longitudinal study.** Ten-day quit attempt with up to 16 prompts/day; 46 enrolled, 38 met EMA adherence criteria, and 30 also met sensor criteria. Includes craving, affect, quit confidence/motivation, context, caffeine, nicotine use, and sensors. Data and analytic code are archived; inspect the codebook, exact files, and license before reuse. The 2026 Leppin analysis reuses this cohort. | Evaluate cigarette-lapse models and group, individual, and hybrid approaches. Strong methodological overlap, but momentary cigarette-lapse measures differ from daily pouch-use and anxiety scores. |
| [Fisher (2019), harmonized by openESM](https://openesmdata.org/datasets/0037_fisher/) — Aaron J. Fisher and Peter D. Soyster; study reference 2019. [Harmonized archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17348069) · [Original data/code](https://osf.io/nkemg/). | **Real smoking EMA dataset.** The harmonized record reports 52 regular smokers, four randomly scheduled prompts/day, and maxima of 128 observations and 34 days; these are not a total row count or an identical schedule for every person. Includes cigarette use, craving, stress, nervousness, restlessness, irritability, sleep, and social experience. Many ratings use 0–100 scales; some sleep wording/coding is uncertain. Harmonized license: CC BY-NC 4.0. | Support personalized smoking research and reuse of repeated psychological/behavioral measurements. Close variable overlap; cigarette counts and momentary ratings require careful comparison with daily pouch measures. |
| [Smoke Free app lapse-report data](https://datacompass.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/3694/) — Olga Perski, David Simons, and collaborators; data record 2023. [Data/code archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8423920) · [Study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntad051). | **Real observational app data, separate from EMASENS.** The study reports 37,002 self-initiated entries from 791 users with at least 20 entries each; 7.6% were lapse reports. Includes craving, mood, activity, and social context. The institutional record points to an archive; file contents and reuse terms were not inspected in this review. | Classify lapse versus non-lapse reports and compare population, individual, and hybrid models. Self-selected app entries are not regularly sampled person-days or automatically prospective forecasts. |
| [Daily smoking and decision-making study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-026-02448-5) — Jeung-Hyun Lee and collaborators; 2026. [Analysis data/code](https://github.com/CCS-Lab/SmokingPred_ADO). | **Real, completed study.** Seventy-nine cessation-clinic participants followed for 5–6 weeks, averaging 23.3 daily observations. Includes smoking, craving, stress, mood, anxiety, medication adherence, and decision-making tasks. The paper links analysis data/code; the repository contents and license need file-level review before reuse. | Predict next-day cigarette consumption and end-of-treatment cessation. Especially relevant to daily forecasting, while product, treatment setting, and cognitive measurements differ. |
| [Smart-T2 secondary-analysis cohort](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2020.108340) — Emily T. Hébert and collaborators; 2021. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8496911/). | **Real clinic-based cohort.** Analysis of 74 participants asked to complete five EMAs/day for four post-quit weeks; 52 had an identified lapse and 22 were classified as non-lapsed. Measures include urge, confidence, motivation, and cigarette availability. Public participant-level data/code and reuse conditions were not verified. | Identify predictors of time to first smoking lapse. Related repeated-measures structure, but an event-time outcome differs from continuous daily craving regression. |
| [mCrave smoking-cessation sensor data](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5161415/) — Soujanya Chatterjee and collaborators; 2016. [Paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2971648.2971672). | **Real, completed sensor study.** Reports 2,012 sensor-hours and 1,812 craving reports from 61 participants; the model's leave-one-participant-out analysis uses a smaller eligible sample. Physiological stress and time-of-day information support minute-level craving estimation. A reusable data/code archive was not verified. | Estimate craving continuously between sparse self-reports. Useful for sensor and unseen-person evaluation ideas, with a much finer time scale than daily records. |

## 3. Related literature

These studies support the scientific background or evaluation design. Association analyses, completed predictive evaluations, and methodological work are identified separately. No source below has been verified as using the exact Kaggle dataset.

| Work / Dataset and Authors | Brief Description: Methods and Results | Research Objective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Rapid and reliable computational markers of decision-making for predicting daily smoking behavior and smoking cessation treatment outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-026-02448-5) — Jeung-Hyun Lee and collaborators; *Neuropsychopharmacology*, 2026. [Data/code](https://github.com/CCS-Lab/SmokingPred_ADO). | **Completed predictive study; Lee cohort in Section 2.** Uses time-lagged mixed-effects models for next-day cigarette count and models of cessation. Reports test R-squared 0.552 for a comprehensive model under standard cross-validation; forward-chaining results differ, including 0.483 for a use/craving model. Extra EMA variables did not uniformly improve performance. | Assess predictive value and measurement burden of daily self-reports and cognitive tasks; a close precedent for daily prediction and incremental feature comparisons. |
| [Supervised machine learning to predict smoking lapses from Ecological Momentary Assessments and sensor data: Implications for just-in-time adaptive intervention development](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0000594) — Olga Perski and collaborators; *PLOS Digital Health*, 2024. [Data/code](https://github.com/OlgaPerski/EMASENS_ML). | **Completed predictive study; EMASENS cohort.** Compares group, individual, and hybrid algorithms with/without sensors. Without sensors, reports pooled group AUC 0.899 but median unseen-person AUC 0.639; individual models were feasible for 15/38 participants. Different evaluation summaries and eligible samples limit direct model comparisons. | Identify feasible lapse-classification approaches for a future JITAI and assess generalization to individuals. |
| [Optimising supervised machine learning algorithms predicting cigarette cravings and lapses for a smoking cessation just-in-time adaptive intervention (JITAI)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0349028) — Corinna Leppin and collaborators; *PLOS One*, 2026. [Linked study repository](https://github.com/OlgaPerski/EMASENS_ML) · [Analysis materials](https://osf.io/tnu72/). | **Completed secondary analysis of the same EMASENS cohort, not a new dataset.** Uses 5,964 EMA responses from 37 participants to vary prompt frequency, predictor count, and participant-specific training data. Reports median ROC-AUC 0.659 for lapses and 0.628 for high craving across configurations. Feature reduction and personalization effects depend on target and metric. | Balance measurement burden and model performance; directly relevant to feature comparisons and personalization, which are already studied approaches. |
| [Classification of Lapses in Smokers Attempting to Stop: A Supervised Machine Learning Approach Using Data From a Popular Smoking Cessation Smartphone App](https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntad051) — Olga Perski and collaborators; *Nicotine & Tobacco Research*, 2023. [Data/code record](https://datacompass.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/3694/). | **Completed predictive classification; Smoke Free cohort.** Reports pooled group AUC 0.969, with unseen-person AUCs ranging 0.482–1.000. Individual models were feasible for 39/791 users and hybrid models for 184/791. Classifies reported lapses; do not assume this is a forecast before an event. | Compare group, individual, and hybrid classification using real-world, self-initiated app entries. |
| [Predicting the first smoking lapse during a quit attempt: A machine learning approach](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2020.108340) — Emily T. Hébert and collaborators; *Drug and Alcohol Dependence*, 2021. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8496911/). | **Completed exploratory survival modeling; Smart-T2 cohort.** Uses Cox models, elastic-net selection, and backward reduction. Five retained predictors include perceived likelihood of smoking, motivation, confidence, urge, and cigarette availability. The reduced model did not adequately approximate the fuller baseline model; a comparable external predictive benchmark was not established in this review. | Identify parsimonious predictors of time to first lapse; useful for feature-selection and outcome-definition decisions. |
| [mCrave: Continuous Estimation of Craving During Smoking Cessation](https://doi.org/10.1145/2971648.2971672) — Soujanya Chatterjee and collaborators; *UbiComp*, 2016. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5161415/). | **Completed predictive estimation; mCrave cohort.** Uses a conditional random-field approach with sensor-derived stress and temporal information, evaluated using leave-one-participant-out cross-validation. Sparse craving labels and individual variability limit minute-level estimation. | Estimate current craving between self-reports, illustrating a task distinct from forecasting a later daily outcome. |
| [Generating accurate personalized predictions of future behavior: A smoking exemplar](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/e24v6) — Aaron J. Fisher and Peter D. Soyster; 2019 preprint. [Dataset record](https://openesmdata.org/datasets/0037_fisher/) · [OSF data/code](https://osf.io/nkemg/). | **Personalized-prediction precedent; Fisher cohort.** The openESM record links this preprint and the underlying materials. Exact model specifications, forecast horizon, numerical results, and subsequent publication status require full-text verification. | Investigate personalized smoking prediction from repeated observations; prioritize for deeper reading before making a novelty claim. |
| [Modeling Idiographic Longitudinal Relationships between Affect and Cigarette Use: An Ecological Momentary Assessment Study](https://doi.org/10.1080/10826084.2023.2257312) — Joseph A. Spillane and Peter Soyster; *Substance Use & Misuse*, 2023. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37735802/). | **Completed association study.** Fifty-two participants completed four smartphone EMAs/day for 30 days. Individual generalized linear models show that affect–craving/smoking associations vary in direction and strength across people. Relationship to the Fisher/openESM cohort is unverified; do not count it as a confirmed independent dataset. | Characterize person-specific affect and smoking relationships; motivates examining heterogeneity without equating association with held-out prediction. |
| [The relationship between nicotine craving and state anxiety in daily and non-daily nicotine users](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2025.108501) — Arwynn A. McKinty, Laura E. Hatz, Weichen Liu, Gianna Andrade, Neal Doran, and Kelly E. Courtney; *Addictive Behaviors*, 2026. | **Completed laboratory association study.** Eighty daily and 34 non-daily nicotine/tobacco users completed VR cue exposure and anxiety/craving assessments. Reports a positive association between state anxiety and pre-cue craving; post-cue relationships depend on use group. Data available on request per publisher. | Examine how anxiety–craving relationships depend on use frequency and cue exposure. State anxiety and laboratory craving are not automatically equivalent to the Kaggle daily scores. |
| [Associations between nicotine product use and craving among stable daily and non-daily users](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2023.107803) — Brandon W. Reed, Neal Doran, and Kelly E. Courtney; *Addictive Behaviors*, 2023. [Institutional full text](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/68r608rd). | **Completed longitudinal association study.** Assesses craving and use twice daily for seven days in 80 daily and 34 non-daily combustible-tobacco/e-cigarette users; uses negative-binomial regression. Potential participant overlap with McKinty et al. requires confirmation; matching sample sizes alone are insufficient evidence. | Study craving–use relationships in active users outside a quit attempt; useful for distinguishing use patterns and product contexts. |
| [Using ecological momentary assessment to assess the temporal relationship between sleep quality and cravings in individuals recovering from substance use disorders](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2017.11.001) — Lindsey K. Freeman and Nisha C. Gottfredson; *Addictive Behaviors*, 2018. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29137841/). | **Completed multilevel association study.** N=122 treatment participants, four surveys/day for three weeks. Examines sleep, craving, and perceived willpower; reports temporal associations in both directions and important between-person differences. This is not a nicotine-pouch-specific forecasting benchmark; data/code availability unverified. | Examine sleep–craving dynamics and distinguish within-person daily changes from differences between people. |
| [Study protocol: Using ecological momentary assessment and wearable sensors to examine mechanisms linking sleep and smoking cessation among adults who are socioeconomically disadvantaged](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0334129) — Chaelin K. Ra and collaborators; *PLOS One*, 2025. | **Proposed study protocol, not completed results.** Plans six weeks of participation, five daily EMAs, and wearable sleep measures spanning pre-quit and post-quit periods. No outcome findings should be inferred from its hypotheses. | Examine bidirectional sleep, withdrawal, abstinence, and smoking relationships; useful for future measurement design. |
| [A randomised, open-label, cross-over clinical study to evaluate the pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic and safety and tolerability profiles of tobacco-free oral nicotine pouches relative to cigarettes](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-022-06178-6) — Fiona Chapman and collaborators; *Psychopharmacology*, 2022. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9217727/). | **Completed controlled product study.** N=24; compares two pouch strengths with cigarettes using nicotine pharmacokinetics and subjective measures. Reports slower nicotine peaks for the tested pouches than cigarettes. Authors are affiliated with Imperial Brands or its subsidiary; assess this context and study conditions. Provides product-specific context rather than a daily ML dataset. | Understand why nicotine pouch strength/use cannot be treated as equivalent to cigarette counts or delivery timing. |
| [Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856/) — Sayash Kapoor and Arvind Narayanan; *Patterns*, 2023. | **Methodological study.** Develops a taxonomy of leakage in collection, preprocessing, modeling, and evaluation, with documented examples across research fields. No nicotine prediction results. | Guide checks of target construction, predictor availability, preprocessing within folds, and dependence between training and evaluation records. |
| [A framework for understanding label leakage in machine learning for health care](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocad178) — Sharon E. Davis, Michael E. Matheny, Suresh Balu, and Mark P. Sendak; *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association*, 2023 publication date in [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37669138/). | **Methodological framework.** Explains why healthcare label leakage requires attention to prediction context as well as temporal order. Full-text details remain to be reviewed; no nicotine-specific benchmark. | Define the intended prediction moment and determine whether each predictor is legitimately available then. |
| [On the fidelity versus privacy and utility trade-off of synthetic patient data](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2025.112382) — Tim Adams and collaborators; *iScience*, 2025. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12059695/) · [Fidelity-evaluation code](https://github.com/SCAI-BIO/syndat). | **Completed synthetic-data methodology study.** Evaluates fidelity, downstream utility, and privacy across five synthetic-data models and three patient-level datasets. Its clinical datasets are not established sources of the Kaggle data. Exact methods and applicability require full-text review. | Inform limitations if synthetic provenance is confirmed; internal predictive accuracy alone does not establish usefulness for real nicotine behavior. |
